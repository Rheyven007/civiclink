PROJECT PROPOSAL
CivicLink: Inclusive Urban Governance and Participation System
Revised edition — aligned with the implemented system (v4)
1. Introduction
1.1 Description
CivicLink: Inclusive Urban Governance and Participation System is a centralized web
based platform for communication, participation, service request management, 
complaint filing, and transparent decision-making between citizens, sector 
representatives, and Local Government Unit (LGU) personnel.
The system provides a single platform for citizens to register, verify their identity, 
manage their profile, submit community project proposals, vote on and discuss 
proposals, request non-emergency public services, file complaints and disputes, 
participate in public consultations, receive notifications, and provide feedback regarding 
government services.
CivicLink provides a dedicated environment for sector representatives to review sector
related issues and endorse community proposals. LGU officers are provided with case 
management tools for proposals, service requests, and complaints, including decision 
recording, performance monitoring via Service Level Agreement (SLA) tracking, and 
analytical reports. The system administrators manage the users, identity verification, 
sectors, consultation content, system settings, reports, moderation activities, and audit 
logs.
The system emphasizes transparency and accountability by requiring LGU officers to 
provide written justifications when recording decisions on proposals, service requests, 
and complaints. These decision records become part of the system's accountability 
mechanism.
CivicLink also supports legal compliance and inclusion. Users accept the Terms of Use 
and the Data Privacy Notice (Republic Act No. 10173) before using the system, every 
submission form shows the laws that apply to it, and users can adjust accessibility 
settings such as text size, contrast, and motion.
CivicLink is implemented as a web-based information system and does not utilize IoT 
devices, sensors, or sensor-based monitoring technologies.
2. Background of the Project
Local government units handle various concerns involving community projects, public services, 
citizen complaints, and community participation. However, when these processes are reliant on 
separate channels and manual procedures, it can be difficult for citizens to gauge the progress 
of their concerns, and for LGU personnel to keep centralized records and monitor pending 
cases.
Moreover, citizen participation can be limited when there is no centralized platform through 
which they can propose community projects, participate in consultations, or provide feedback 
regarding public services.
CivicLink addresses the aforementioned issues by providing a centralized web-based system 
which combines citizen participation and LGU case management.
Through the system, citizens can submit and monitor their concerns, participate in proposals 
and consultations, and receive updates. LGU officers can manage submitted cases, record 
decisions and justifications, and monitor service performance. Sector representatives can help 
amplify the community's concerns and endorse proposals, and administrators can manage the 
system's data and monitor activities.
3. Problem Statement
The development of CivicLink aims to address the following problems:
3.1 Difficulty in Tracking Government Requests
Citizens may have difficulty monitoring the progress of service requests, proposals, and 
complaints when there is no centralized tracking mechanism.
3.2 Limited Citizen Participation
Citizens may have limited opportunities to participate in community planning and 
decision-making processes.
3.3 Fragmented Management of Citizen Concerns
Proposals, service requests, and complaints may be handled through separate 
processes, making monitoring and management difficult.
3.4 Lack of Transparent Decision Documentation
Citizens may not clearly understand why a proposal, request, or complaint was 
approved, rejected, escalated, or closed.
3.5 Limited Representation of Community Sectors
Sector-specific concerns may not be sufficiently represented in community planning and 
proposal evaluation.
3.6 Difficulty Monitoring LGU Performance
Without centralized performance information, monitoring service processing and 
resolution times can be difficult.
3.7 Limited Feedback Mechanisms
Citizens may have insufficient mechanisms for evaluating public services after their 
concerns have been addressed.
3.8 Limited Awareness of Rights and Responsibilities
Citizens may not know which laws protect their personal data and govern online 
conduct, and LGUs need proof that users agreed to the terms of the platform.
3.9 Barriers to Inclusive Access
Persons with disabilities, senior citizens, and users on mobile phones may find 
government systems hard to read and navigate.
4. Proposed Solution
CivicLink will provide a centralized web-based platform which integrates the major citizen
government interaction processes into one system.
The proposed solution consists of four major user environments:
Citizen Portal → Sector Representative Portal → LGU Officer Portal → 
Administrator Portal
The Citizen Portal allows residents to submit proposals, service requests, complaints, 
participate in consultations, voting and discussions, monitor notifications, and provide 
feedback.
The Sector Representative Portal provides additional tools for viewing sector-related 
concerns and endorsing community proposals.
The LGU Officer Portal provides centralized case management for proposals, service 
requests, and complaints, including decision documentation, SLA/performance 
monitoring, and reports.
The Administrator Portal provides tools for managing users, identity verification, and 
sectors, moderating consultations, configuring SLA targets, generating reports, and 
reviewing audit logs.
All portals share a Laws & Terms center, a Help Center with a guided tour, global 
search, in-system notifications, and accessibility settings.
5. General Objective
To develop CivicLink, a centralized web-based governance and participation system that 
enables citizens to participate in community decision-making, submit and track public concerns, 
and promotes transparent and accountable management of proposals, service requests, and 
complaints by LGU personnel.
6. Specific Objectives
The system specifically aims to:
 Develop a secure registration and login system supporting different user roles.
 Implement role-based access for Citizens, Sector Representatives, LGU Officers, and 
Administrators.
 Verify citizen identity through the upload of a government-issued ID that administrators 
review.
 Provide citizens with a centralized dashboard (Community Activity Hub) for accessing 
governance-related services.
 Enable citizens to submit community project proposals containing descriptions, 
justifications, expected benefits, and categories.
 Provide proposal voting through support and oppose mechanisms, and structured 
discussion through comments.
 Enable sector representatives to endorse community proposals.
 Provide a centralized service request system for non-emergency public concerns, with 
location and photo attachments.
 Develop a complaint and dispute filing system that allows citizens to submit supporting 
evidence.
 Enable LGU officers to manage proposals, service requests, and complaints through a 
centralized case management interface.
 Require LGU officers to provide written justifications when recording decisions.
 Provide citizens with transparent status information and a progress timeline for their 
submissions.
 Develop a public consultation mechanism where citizens can provide responses and 
indicate support, opposition, or neutrality.
 Provide notification functionality for important system and case updates, grouped by 
category.
 Implement a feedback and rating mechanism for completed services and cases.
 Provide LGU officers with SLA and performance monitoring tools, with targets 
configurable by administrators.
 Provide administrators with user, sector, moderation, settings, reporting, and audit 
management functions.
 Generate analytical reports that summarize proposals, service requests, complaints, 
consultations, and other system activities, exportable to CSV and print/PDF.
 Record each user's acceptance of the Terms of Use and Data Privacy Notice, and 
inform users of applicable Philippine laws.
 Provide a help center, a guided tour, global search, and accessibility settings so that all 
residents can use the system.
7. Significance of the Project
Citizens
CivicLink gives citizens a centralized platform for submitting community proposals, 
requesting public services, filing complaints, participating in consultations, and 
monitoring the progress of their submissions.
Sector Representatives
The system allows sector representatives to monitor sector-related concerns and 
endorse community proposals, helping strengthen representation in local governance.
LGU Officers
LGU personnel can manage citizen submissions through a centralized case 
management system, record decisions, provide written justifications, and monitor service 
performance.
System Administrators
Administrators can manage users and sectors, moderate consultations, generate 
reports, and review audit logs to support system integrity and accountability.
Community Organizations
Community organizations and representatives can use participation mechanisms to 
support proposals and contribute to discussions regarding community concerns.
Persons with Disabilities and Senior Citizens
Accessibility settings (larger text, high contrast, reduced motion, underlined links, 
readable spacing) make the system easier to use for residents with visual, motor, or 
cognitive needs.
Future Researchers
The system can serve as a reference for future studies involving digital governance, 
citizen participation, transparency, public service management, and inclusive 
technology.
8. Scope of the System
The actual CivicLink implementation covers the following areas.
8.1 Authentication and User Management
The system provides:
 User registration (citizens), with optional upload of a valid government-issued ID
 Login and logout, with a "Keep me signed in" option
 Role-based access
 Account management, including creation of staff and citizen accounts by 
administrators
 User status management (Active, Pending, Suspended)
 Profile management (name, contact number, address, barangay, sector, photo, 
short bio)
 Password change by the user and password reset by administrators
 Identity verification: administrators review the uploaded ID and mark the citizen as 
Verified; verified users show a verified badge
 Acceptance of the Terms of Use and Data Privacy Notice on first sign-in and 
whenever the terms are updated
 Login protection: repeated failed attempts are temporarily blocked
The implemented roles are:
 Citizen
 Sector Representative
 LGU Officer
 Administrator
Quick sign-in (evaluation accounts): the login page can list one active account per role, 
read from the users table, so that evaluators and panelists can sign in with one click. 
This option is controlled by the QUICK_LOGIN setting in api/config.php and must be 
turned off when the system is deployed for public use.
8.2 Citizen Dashboard (Community Activity Hub)
The Citizen Dashboard serves as the central interface for citizens. It is organized as a 
Community Activity Hub with:
 Quick actions: Request a service, Raise a concern, Propose an initiative
 A community status board: open consultations, proposals under review, the user's 
active cases, official decisions recorded in the last 30 days, and the request 
resolution rate
 A Community ↔ Government workflow showing how submissions move from 
residents to the community, the local government, and resolution
 A civic bulletin of open consultations and recent official decisions
 Typed activity cards for community proposals, public consultations, and official 
decision notices
 A civic alert bar that shows real alerts only (for example, an open consultation, or 
overdue cases for staff)
 A side panel with the user's civic record, proposals gaining community support, 
open consultations, and active discussions
Citizens can access:
 Proposals
 Service requests
 Complaints
 Consultations
 Notifications
 Feedback
 Profile management
 Global search across proposals, cases, and reference numbers (e.g., REQ-0002)
8.3 Community Proposal System
Citizens can create community proposals containing:
 Proposal title
 Description
 Justification
 Expected benefits
 Category
 Photo (optional)
The system supports proposal statuses including:
Pending → Under Review → Approved / Rejected → Implemented
Citizens can also vote on proposals using:
 Support
 Oppose
Additional proposal features:
 Discussion: citizens, sector representatives, and LGU staff can post comments on 
a proposal; authors and administrators can delete comments.
 Withdrawal: the author can withdraw a proposal while it is still Pending.
 Progress and support level: each proposal shows its current stage, the share of 
residents in support, and the number of sector endorsements.
 Copy link: a direct link to a proposal can be copied and shared.
8.4 Sector Representative System
Sector representatives have additional functions beyond regular citizen participation. 
They can:
 View sector-related issues through a Sector Hub, filtered by sector
 Monitor community concerns (service requests and complaints are listed, but 
private details such as descriptions, locations, and attachments of other citizens 
are hidden)
 View pending proposals
 Endorse proposals
 Participate in consultations
The endorsement mechanism allows sector representatives to support proposals on 
behalf of the sector they represent.
8.5 Public Service Request System
Citizens can submit non-emergency service requests. Each request contains information 
such as:
 Service type
 Description
 Location
 Photo attachment (optional; JPG, PNG, WEBP, or PDF up to 5 MB)
 Department
 Priority
 Assigned LGU personnel
 Status
The implemented request statuses include:
Submitted → In Progress → Resolved → Closed
The system also supports cancellation by the citizen while a request is still Submitted, 
with a reason. Each request shows an SLA meter (time elapsed against the target for its 
priority) and a progress timeline.
8.6 Complaint and Dispute System
Citizens can file complaints and disputes by providing:
 Subject
 Details
 Category
 Supporting evidence
Complaints can be assigned to LGU personnel and processed through statuses such as:
Filed → Investigating → Mediation → Resolved / Dismissed
Resolution notes can also be recorded. Complaint details and evidence are visible only 
to the complainant and LGU staff.
8.7 LGU Case Management
LGU officers have a unified case management interface covering:
 Community proposals
 Service requests
 Complaints and disputes
Officers can update case statuses, assign cases to personnel, set priority, record 
decisions, and export case lists to CSV. Each case has a vertical progress timeline and 
a decision log.
This provides a centralized workflow rather than requiring separate systems for each 
type of citizen concern.
9. Transparent Decision Mechanism
One of the key features of CivicLink is its decision justification mechanism.
When an LGU officer records a decision regarding a proposal, service request, or complaint, the 
system requires a written justification.
Decision records include:
 Reference type
 Reference ID
 Decision
 Status change (from status → to status)
 Responsible LGU officer
 Written justification
 Date and time
Decisions on proposals are published to the community as Official Civic Notices that show the 
deciding office, the decision, and the quoted justification. This creates a traceable decision 
history and strengthens the system's transparency and accountability objectives.
10. SLA and Performance Monitoring
The system includes an SLA & Performance module for LGU officers. It provides information 
related to:
 Total service requests
 Resolved requests
 Average resolution time
 Overdue requests
 SLA compliance rate
 Department performance
The system can identify service requests that remain unresolved beyond the defined processing 
threshold. The threshold for each priority (Urgent, High, Normal, Low) is set by the administrator 
in System Settings and stored in the database; the default targets follow the processing times of 
RA 11032 (Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act).
This enables LGU personnel to monitor operational performance and identify areas requiring 
improvement.
11. Public Consultation System
Citizens can participate in consultations created within the system. A consultation contains:
 Title
 Description
 Start date
 End date
 Status (Open / Closed)
Citizens can submit responses and indicate their position through:
 Support
 Oppose
 Neutral
Citizens may edit their own response while the consultation is open. Results are shown as a 
tally of positions. This module provides a structured mechanism for collecting citizen input on 
community-related matters.
12. Feedback and Rating System
After receiving services or having cases addressed, users can provide feedback through:
 Rating
 Comments
The rating system uses a 1–5 rating scale. Feedback can refer to a specific service request, 
complaint, or to the system in general. Aggregated feedback can assist LGU personnel and 
administrators in identifying potential areas for service improvement.
13. Notification System
CivicLink provides in-system notifications to keep users informed about relevant activities. 
Notifications may inform users about:
 Proposal updates
 Service request updates
 Complaint updates
 Consultation activities
 Voting-related changes
 Sector endorsements
 New comments on the user's proposals
 Other system events (e.g., new registrations needing ID verification, for administrators)
Notifications are grouped into categories — Government, Reports & cases, Community, 
Discussions, Consultations, and System — and can be filtered by category or by unread status, 
opened directly, and marked as read individually or all at once. The number of unread 
notifications is shown on the bell icon.
14. Administrator Management
The Administrator Dashboard provides system-level management functions. Administrators can 
manage:
User Management
 View users
 Create accounts
 Manage accounts
 Manage user statuses
 Manage roles
 Reset passwords
 Review uploaded IDs and verify citizens
Sector Management
 Manage registered sectors
 Maintain sector information
Consultation Moderation
 Create, edit, open/close, and delete consultations
 Review and moderate consultation content (hide or delete responses)
System Settings
 Configure SLA targets per priority
 Export a JSON backup of the data visible to the administrator
Reports and Analytics
 View system statistics
 Generate reports
 Analyze system activities
Audit Logs
 Review recorded system activities (sign-ins, decisions, status changes, terms 
acceptance, tutorial completion)
 Monitor administrative and governance-related actions
 Export audit logs to CSV
15. Reports and Analytics
The Reports & Analytics module is available to Administrators and LGU Officers. It computes all 
figures from the existing database records; no separate reporting table is required.
 Reporting period: 7 days, 30 days, 90 days, or all time, compared with the previous 
period of the same length.
 Executive summary: key figures for proposals, service requests, complaints, 
consultation responses, active participants, and average citizen rating, each with its 
change and a small trend line.
 Activity trend: a chart of submissions over the selected period.
 Insights: automatically generated observations (for example, the busiest category or a 
drop in resolution rate).
 Participation: the proposal pipeline, participation by sector, and participation by 
barangay.
 Service delivery: SLA compliance and resolution-rate gauges, satisfaction rating, and a 
department scorecard.
 Accountability: decisions recorded with justification and a ranking of officers by cases 
handled.
 Compliance: an RA 11032 processing-time compliance table and related legal 
statements.
 Export: summary CSV download and a print-ready layout that can be saved as PDF.
16. Laws, Terms and Data Privacy Compliance
CivicLink includes a Laws & Terms center (also viewable without signing in) containing the 
Terms of Use, a Privacy Notice under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173), and summaries 
of the laws that apply to the platform.
 Terms acceptance: on first sign-in, and again whenever the terms version changes, the 
user must agree to the Terms of Use, consent to personal-data processing, and 
acknowledge the rules on online conduct. Each acceptance is stored in the 
terms_acceptances table (user, terms version, IP address, date and time) and written to 
the audit log.
 Registration consent: registration requires agreement to the Data Privacy notice.
 Form notices: each submission form shows the laws relevant to it and requires a 
confirmation checkbox.
 Community reminder: a short reminder about respectful and lawful participation 
appears once per session.

17. Help Center and Guided Tour
 Guided tour: starts automatically for first-time users and highlights the main features 
step by step, with separate steps for each role. Completion is saved to the user's 
account (user_preferences table) and recorded in the audit log.
 Help Center: can be opened from any page; it offers quick jumps to features, a replay of 
the tour, and frequently asked questions.
18. Accessibility and Personalization
Accessibility settings are optional and off by default. They can be opened from the header or the 
account menu, and the user's choices are saved to their account (user_preferences table) so 
that they apply on any device.
 Text size (Default, Large, Extra large)
 Light or dark appearance
 High contrast
 Reduced motion (turns off animations and transitions)
 Underlined links
 Readable spacing
The interface also provides a skip-to-content link, visible keyboard focus, labelled dialogs that 
keep keyboard focus inside, and color combinations that meet WCAG AA contrast.
19. User Interface Design
CivicLink uses a civic design system rather than a social-media layout:
 Semantic colors for community, government, emergency, information, success, and 
warning content
 An official civic strip (LGU notice, Laws & Terms, and an emergency 911 reminder) and 
a header with community context, global search, Report a Concern, accessibility, help, 
notifications, and profile
 Navigation grouped by activity: Community, Services, and My Activity for residents; 
Operations and Administration for staff
 Consistent primary, secondary, tertiary, and destructive buttons; loading placeholders; 
and empty states that suggest the next action
 Short transitions and page-entry animations (150–400 ms) that are disabled when the 
user or the device requests reduced motion
 Responsive layouts for desktop, tablet, and mobile; on phones the system uses a bottom 
tab bar with a central Report button, a slide-out menu, bottom-sheet dialogs that can be 
swiped down to close, and tables that turn into cards
20. System Architecture and Database Design
CivicLink is a single-page web application written in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. It 
communicates with a PHP 8 JSON API (api/index.php) that uses PDO to access a MySQL or 
MariaDB database. Sessions are protected by HTTP-only cookies; passwords are stored with 
password_hash; uploaded files are type-checked and limited to 5 MB. The system requires the 
web server and database to be running; it has no offline or browser-only mode.

21. Limitations
 CivicLink handles non-emergency concerns only; emergencies must be reported through 
911.
 The system does not use IoT devices, sensors, or sensor-based monitoring.
 Notifications are delivered inside the system only (no SMS or e-mail).
 The system requires an internet or local-network connection to the web server and 
database.
 Quick sign-in is intended for evaluation only and must be disabled in production.
